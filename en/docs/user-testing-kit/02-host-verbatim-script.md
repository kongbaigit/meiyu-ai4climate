# Host Verbatim Script

**Version:** `UT-20261016-v1`. Host-only; contains answers. Prepare the scoring rules and blank individual record. Do not send this file to participants.

## Before the test (not timed)

Check the device and reset the page using the start-here checklist. Participants must operate independently; a host screen demo is rehearsal only. Record device, open method, date, and demo digest. Close unrelated windows; remotely share only the prototype window and never capture other apps.

Know the two cases but do not disclose answers: June 1 probability 67.9%, raw forecast 52.5 mm, SSOD reference 29.0 mm; July 18 probability about 2.8%, raw forecast 5.7 mm, SSOD reference 101.8 mm.

## 0-1 minute: explanation and consent

Read the consent text and obtain clear agreement. Ask: “Do you have experience with stock, moisture, or rain preparation? You may decline to answer.” Record only yes/no/prefer not to answer.

Say: “Please follow the task card and talk through what you are thinking. You can say you do not know, cannot find it, or do not want to continue. I will observe first and not explain the answer immediately.”

## 1-4 minutes: main task, no more than 180 seconds

Give the participant task card, **Part A only**. After they read it, confirm start and begin timing; task-card reading is outside the 180 seconds.

“Please inspect the historical window starting 2025-06-01 at 08:00, tell me the probability, find the hypothetical preparation cost and explain what CNY 10 means, reveal the later reference precipitation for that same window, and find the source and one limitation.”

Record T1-T5 completion, cumulative time, and help. If silent, say only “You can continue in your own way.” Repeating the task is not help. Do not point to controls or answer. The participant need not open an external link when the page shows the source.

If the participant asks for help, first ask whether they want another attempt or a hint. If they request a hint, help is allowed but independent completion ends at the first specific hint; record assisted completion. If a page, connection, or content fault blocks execution, end the first timer and record interruption seconds; affected incomplete T items are X and unanswered Q items are U. Do not pause and later call it a first success. Failing to find a button is not a technical fault, so time continues. At 180 seconds, say “Thank you, we will stop here for now.” Record incomplete items; 180 seconds is not a completed-task duration.

## 4-7 minutes: first comprehension questions

Let the participant view the page. Ask the exact questions, repeating them if needed but not adding answers. One neutral “Can you say a little more?” prompt is allowed per question. Separate first answers from post-explanation answers.

1. “What exactly is this probability estimating? Can it tell you whether the shop will flood?”
2. “If the displayed probability is low, how would you interpret it? Can you conclude that it will not happen?”
3. “What does the hypothetical CNY 10 amount mean? Can you treat it as your actual spending or money already saved?”
4. “From what Beijing time does this date start, and when does it end?”
5. “Open Latest weather and completeness. What role does ERA5 play here? Can it fill a station precipitation-reference gap?”
6. “Point to the information showing when the data was retrieved and the current cache status. How would you decide whether it counts as latest weather?”

Switching to Latest weather is a required step, not help. Pointing to an answer or operating for the participant makes Q5/Q6 assisted. Q6 observes the existing cache and does not click refresh. A non-stale cache can be answered correctly without saying “stale”.

## 7-9 minutes: miss case and feedback

Say: “Next is another historical case; your previous answers are already recorded.” Switch to 2025-07-18 at 08:00, keep the hypothetical cost at CNY 10, show about 2.8%, then reveal 101.8 mm. Host operation here is a required demonstration and not part of the main task.

Ask how the result changes the participant's understanding of capability and limits. Record the unprompted answer, then explain that low probability is not a guarantee. Ask what was hardest to understand or find and whether they would open it again in a similar situation and why. Do not ask whether it was useful or imply a preferred answer. Willingness is intention, not adoption or payment evidence.

## 9-10 minutes: explanation and close

Say: “This estimates the probability that reference-day precipitation reaches 25 mm, not shop flooding and not an official warning. The window is Beijing 08:00 to the next day 08:00, not midnight to midnight. A low probability event can still happen. CNY 10 is a hypothetical cost scenario, not measured spending or savings; complete loss avoidance is an assumption. SSOD is a limited derived station reference and ERA5 is reanalysis background, not a station label. Check cache retrieval time and status; opening a page does not make old data current.”

Confirm any quote using separate consent, give the ID and withdrawal channel/deadline, and record total time. If it exceeds 10 minutes, record that fact rather than cutting off an explanation.

## Exceptions and closeout

- **Withdrawal:** stop immediately. Ask whether already written material may remain. Explicit permission keeps the record in $N$; refusal or ambiguity means delete the individual record and exclude it from $N$, retaining only anonymous process counts.
- **Technical interruption:** record fault, step, and exposed questions; end first measurement. Answered questions remain; reached-but-unscorable questions are X; not-yet-reached questions are U. Any continuation is a retry, not first success.
- **Host disclosed an answer:** keep the process record and mark affected items contaminated; do not count first correctness.
- **Review:** same-day scoring; a second reviewer checks the rules against the record rather than memory. Preserve disagreements, original score, review score, and reason. If there is no second reviewer, write “not double-reviewed.”
